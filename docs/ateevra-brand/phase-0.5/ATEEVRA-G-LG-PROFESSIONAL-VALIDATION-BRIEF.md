## 2. Corporate Architecture: Operating Dual Business Lines in One Company

Under the Project Owner's explicit direction recorded on 2026-11-05 ([`ATEEVRA-OD-001-DIR-001`](file:///C:/Users/Shubham/OneDrive/Desktop/ecom_mvp/docs/ateevra-brand/phase-0.5/ATEEVRA-OD-001-CONSOLIDATED-OWNER-DIRECTION-RECORD.md)), the enterprise is structured as **one Indian Private Limited company operating two discrete business lines**:

1. **Business Line 1 — ATEEVRA (First-Party Luxury Fashion Brand — Primary Business Priority):**
   - Direct-to-Consumer (D2C) brand selling exclusively branded apparel and accessories directly to consumers.
   - Company is the direct Seller of Record (SOR), executing customer sales contracts, issuing Section 31 retail invoices, and managing delivery, returns, and customer service.
2. **Business Line 2 — Existing Multi-Vendor Marketplace (Independent Business Line):**
   - Multi-vendor e-commerce platform facilitating sales between independent third-party merchants and consumers.
   - Company acts as an intermediary / electronic commerce platform, earning commission fees.
   - Must continue to exist and operate independently without disruption or destruction of functionality.

### Core Legal Issue for Counsel:
> [!WARNING]
> While internal accounting and operations are segregated into distinct departments, under corporate law, **both business lines share a single corporate legal personality and a single corporate veil**.  
> Any contractual default, labor grievance, product liability claim, regulatory penalty, or tax attachment in one business line attaches directly to the entire company and its directors. Legal counsel must advise on contract drafting and liability ring-fencing to minimize cross-business contamination.

### 3.1 Indian FDI Policy & E-Commerce Business Models
*[PROPOSED REGULATORY INTERPRETATION — SUBJECT TO LEGAL COUNSEL OPINION — NOT CERTIFIED]*
- **Regulatory Framework:** Consolidated FDI Policy Circular of 2020 (Paragraph 5.2.15.2) issued by the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT), read with Press Note 2 (2018 Series) and Press Note 3 (2020 Series).
- **Core Regulatory Restrictions:**
  - *Marketplace Model:* 100% FDI is permitted under the automatic route for marketplace e-commerce platforms. However, an e-commerce entity providing a marketplace is prohibited from exercising ownership or control over the inventory sold on the platform. Inventory of a vendor is deemed to be controlled by the marketplace if more than 25% of purchases of such vendor are from the marketplace entity or its group companies.
  - *Inventory-Based Model:* FDI is **NOT permitted** in inventory-based models of e-commerce (where an e-commerce entity owns the inventory of goods and sells directly to consumers), except under specific Single Brand Retail Trading (SBRT) routes subject to government approvals and local sourcing conditions (30% local sourcing for foreign-invested SBRT entities).
- **Specific Legal Determination Required:**
  1. What is the current foreign shareholding / FDI status of the company? If the company is 100% resident Indian-owned, confirm that FDI restrictions do not apply.
  2. If the company seeks foreign investment or private equity in the future, does operating both an inventory-based 1P brand and a marketplace in the same legal company create an insurmountable FDI compliance violation?
  3. What structural triggers dictate spinning off ATEEVRA into a separate wholly-owned corporate subsidiary prior to taking foreign capital?

### 3.2 Marketplace Intermediary Safe-Harbor vs. 1P Seller of Record
*[PROPOSED REGULATORY INTERPRETATION — SUBJECT TO LEGAL COUNSEL OPINION — NOT CERTIFIED]*
- **Regulatory Framework:** Section 79 of the Information Technology Act, 2000 read with the Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021.
- **Intermediary Safe Harbor Principles:**
  - Under Section 79, a network service provider / platform intermediary is exempt from liability for any third-party information, data, or communication link made available or hosted by it, provided the intermediary does not initiate the transmission, select the receiver, or select/modify the information contained in the transmission, and observes statutory due diligence.
- **Risk Analysis of Single-Company Dual Operation:**
  - Does the company risk losing Section 79 safe-harbor protection for its marketplace business line because the company is also an active merchant / seller of record for its 1P luxury brand?
  - Could a court or consumer forum hold that because the entity is an active apparel merchant, it exercises editorial/commercial control over platform content?
- **Specific Legal Determination Required:** Formulate precise contractual, storefront, and technical separation criteria (e.g., distinct domain names, separate terms of use, clear corporate disclosures) necessary to preserve Section 79 safe-harbor status for Business Line 2.

### 3.3 Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 & Product Liability
*[PROPOSED REGULATORY INTERPRETATION — SUBJECT TO LEGAL COUNSEL OPINION — NOT CERTIFIED]*
- **Regulatory Framework:** Consumer Protection Act, 2019 (specifically Chapter VI: Product Liability) and the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020.
- **Mandatory Compliance Requirements for 1P Brand Operations:**
  - *Duties of Sellers on Marketplace vs. Direct E-Commerce Entities:* ATEEVRA must provide clear disclosures of legal entity name, principal geographic address, customer care number, grievance officer contact, return/refund terms, and country of origin.
  - *Product Liability under Section 84–86:* A product manufacturer and a product seller are strictly liable in a product liability action for harm caused by a defective product (e.g., severe skin rash caused by unapproved chemical textile dyes, physical injury from sharp metal accessories, choking hazard from defective buttons).
  - *Prohibition of Unfair Trade Practices:* Manipulation of price, deceptive reviews, or refusal to accept returns of defective/non-conforming goods is strictly prohibited under Rule 4(4) and Rule 6(3).
- **Specific Legal Determination Required:**
  1. Review the proposed ATEEVRA product liability risk profile and draft necessary customer disclosures, disclaimers, and warranty terms.
  2. Confirm whether the appointment of a single Grievance Officer for both business lines is legally permissible under Rule 4(4), or whether distinct officers must be notified.

### 3.4 Customer Terms of Sale, Concierge Returns & Restocking Deductions
*[PROPOSED REGULATORY INTERPRETATION — SUBJECT TO LEGAL COUNSEL OPINION — NOT CERTIFIED]*
- **Operational Policy Under Review:**
  - ATEEVRA offers a **7-day concierge return window** from the confirmed delivery date.
  - *Simultaneous Size Swap:* ₹0 charge; client receives new size upon courier handover of original item.
  - *Pure Cash Refund:* Allowed within 7 days, subject to garment passing 5-stage triage (unworn, original security tag intact, zero scent/stains).
  - *Restocking Deductions:* ATEEVRA proposes deducting a **flat ₹250.00 reverse logistics & ozone sanitation fee** from the refunded amount when a customer requests a pure cash refund (free for exchanges).
- **Legal Enforceability Consideration:**
  - Under Rule 5(3)(e) of the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020: "No e-commerce entity shall impose cancellation fees on consumers... unless similar charges are also borne by the e-commerce entity, if they cancel the purchase order..."
- **Specific Legal Determination Required:**
  1. Is deducting a ₹250 restocking / reverse shipping deduction legally enforceable under the E-Commerce Rules 2020 and Indian Contract Act, 1872, if clearly disclosed prior to checkout?
  2. Draft the definitive, legally airtight **ATEEVRA Client Terms of Sale & Concierge Return Policy**.

### 3.5 Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011
*[PROPOSED REGULATORY INTERPRETATION — SUBJECT TO LEGAL COUNSEL OPINION — NOT CERTIFIED]*
- **Regulatory Framework:** Legal Metrology Act, 2009 and the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011 (specifically Rule 6(10) governing e-commerce digital storefronts).
- **Mandatory E-Commerce Storefront Declarations (PDP):**
  - Every digital product detail page (PDP) must prominently display:
    1. Name and complete address of the Manufacturer / Packer.
    2. Name of the Country of Origin.
    3. Common or Generic name of the commodity (e.g., "Men's Tailored Blazer").
    4. Net quantity in standard units (e.g., "1 Piece").
    5. Size dimensions (garment measurements: chest, waist, length in cm / inches).
    6. Consumer care details (Name, Address, Phone, Email of the consumer grievance cell).
    7. Maximum Retail Price (MRP) in Indian Rupees, inclusive of all taxes.
- **Mandatory Physical Garment Labeling:**
  - Sewn-in wash care label and exterior swing tag must state: "Manufactured by [Atelier Corporate Name & Address] for and marketed by ATEEVRA LUXURY PRIVATE LIMITED" (or compliant statutory variant).
- **Specific Legal Determination Required:** Audit the proposed ATEEVRA PDP spec sheet and physical swing tag layout to ensure 100% immunity from Legal Metrology inspection seizures and compounding fines.

### 3.6 Master Manufacturing Agreements (MMA) & Atelier Contracting
*[PROPOSED REGULATORY INTERPRETATION — SUBJECT TO LEGAL COUNSEL OPINION — NOT CERTIFIED]*
- **Operational Reality:** ATEEVRA will contract specialized luxury tailoring ateliers in Bangalore and Delhi NCR under contract manufacturing (Option A FOB or Option B CMT).
- **Mandatory Contractual Covenants Required in MMA:**
  1. *Intellectual Property Assignment:* Explicit, irrevocable assignment to ATEEVRA of all copyright in tech packs, 2D/3D pattern geometries, grading curves, and garment silhouettes created by or shared with the atelier.
  2. *Strict Exclusivity & Non-Compete:* Absolute prohibition against producing identical or substantially similar garments for any third-party brand or direct market sale.
  3. *Quality Acceptance & AQL 2.5 Rejection:* Contractual right of ATEEVRA QC inspectors to perform inline and pre-shipment inspections under ISO 2859-1 Level II AQL 2.5, with unilateral right to reject failing lots at the atelier's sole expense.
  4. *Delay Liquidated Damages:* Predetermined damages (e.g., 1.0% of PO value per week of unexcused delay, capped at 10%) to protect seasonal drop delivery windows.
  5. *Labor Law & Factory Act Indemnification:* Absolute indemnification of ATEEVRA against all claims, fines, or liabilities arising under The Factories Act, 1948, The Contract Labour (Regulation and Abolition) Act, 1970, Employees' Provident Funds Act, 1952, and Employees' State Insurance Act, 1948 involving atelier personnel.
- **Specific Legal Determination Required:** Draft the standard **ATEEVRA Master Manufacturing Agreement (B2B Full-Package FOB)** and **Cut-Make-Trim (CMT) Job-Work Agreement**.

### 3.7 Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP Act)
*[PROPOSED REGULATORY INTERPRETATION — SUBJECT TO LEGAL COUNSEL OPINION — NOT CERTIFIED]*
- **Regulatory Framework:** The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (Act No. 22 of 2023).
- **Corporate Obligations:**
  - ATEEVRA processes customer personal data (name, phone, delivery address, sizing measurements, styling preferences, payment instrument identifiers) as a **Data Fiduciary**.
  - Section 6 mandates that processing must be based on explicit, free, specific, informed, and unconditional consent, accompanied by an unambiguous privacy notice in plain language.
  - Section 12 mandates erasure of personal data upon withdrawal of consent or when the specified purpose is fulfilled.
  - Section 8 mandates reasonable security safeguards to prevent personal data breaches, with reporting to the Data Protection Board of India.
- **Specific Legal Determination Required:**
  1. Review the proposed ATEEVRA customer consent capture mechanism during checkout and WhatsApp concierge onboarding.
  2. Formulate the statutory Privacy Notice and Data Retention Policy under DPDP Act 2023.
  3. Validate the administrative PII masking protocol (masking customer phone and payment details in internal customer service tools).

## 4. Concrete Questions Requiring Written Legal Determination

Legal counsel is requested to provide structured, written answers and recommendations for the following 10 questions:

1. **Dual-Model Legal Validity:** Is it legally valid and defensible under Indian law for a single Private Limited company to operate both a 1P inventory-based luxury apparel brand and a multi-vendor marketplace platform? What are the key statutory liabilities and exposure points?
2. **FDI Compliance & Future Equity:** What specific FDI restrictions apply if the company raises foreign investment while operating both models? What timeline or operational triggers necessitate restructuring into a holding company and operating subsidiary?
3. **Section 79 Intermediary Safe Harbor:** Does the company's 1P retail commercial activity compromise its Section 79 IT Act safe-harbor immunity for third-party marketplace transactions? What corporate disclosures and operational separations are required to defend safe harbor?
4. **Consumer Protection E-Commerce Rules:** Does the proposed 1P brand operating model comply with the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020? What specific appointments (Grievance Officer, Nodal Person) are legally required?
5. **Legality of ₹250 Restocking Fee:** Under the E-Commerce Rules 2020, can ATEEVRA legally deduct a ₹250 restocking fee from customer refunds for change-of-mind returns, provided this is disclosed prior to checkout?
6. **Legal Metrology PDP & Label Verification:** What exact wording must appear on the physical garment sewn-in tag, swing tag, and digital PDP to satisfy Rule 6(10) of the Packaged Commodities Rules 2011 when garments are manufactured by contract ateliers?
7. **Atelier IP Ring-Fencing:** What specific clauses must be inserted into Master Manufacturing Agreements to prevent ateliers from using ATEEVRA pattern CAD files, grading tables, or fabric designs to manufacture unauthorized duplicates?
8. **Product Liability & Atelier Pass-Through:** If a customer brings a product liability claim under Chapter VI of the Consumer Protection Act 2019 for textile chemical toxicity or manufacturing defects, how can liability be legally passed through to the manufacturing atelier?
9. **DPDP Act 2023 Compliance:** What specific consent language and privacy notices must be displayed at digital checkout and WhatsApp concierge onboarding to ensure complete DPDP Act compliance?
10. **Slump Sale & Future Subsidiary Carve-Out:** What contractual provisions should be inserted into vendor contracts, lease agreements, and gateway integrations to enable a seamless future business transfer (slump sale) of the ATEEVRA brand into a separate subsidiary without requiring counterparty contract renegotiation?
