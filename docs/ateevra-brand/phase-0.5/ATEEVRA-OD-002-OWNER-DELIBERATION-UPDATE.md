## 1. Executive Summary

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Contractual Definition: Domestic "Full-Package Contract Manufacturing" vs Maritime Incoterms FOB**  
> In this deliberation update and across the ATEEVRA project documentation, the term **"FOB" (Option A)** is utilized strictly in accordance with informal Indian apparel industry terminology to designate a **Full-Package B2B Contract Manufacturing** model—where the contracted atelier is responsible for end-to-end procurement of approved fabrics and trims, pattern cutting, sewing, finishing, and packing to ATEEVRA tech packs.  
> This must be strictly distinguished from the technical maritime Incoterms 2020 meaning of FOB (*Free On Board*), which applies exclusively to sea/inland waterway freight where a seller delivers goods on board a vessel nominated by the buyer at a port of shipment. For domestic Indian operations, all contracts are domestic B2B supply agreements with delivery terms specified as **Ex-Works, FOR Destination, or DDP (Delivered Duty Paid)** to central warehouse `WH01` Bengaluru.


This document provides the structured deliberation analysis for **Owner Decision OD-002**, updated to reflect the Project Owner's strategic commercial direction recorded on 2026-11-05: **operating ATEEVRA as an independent first-party luxury fashion brand (sole Seller of Record) alongside an existing marketplace within a single legal company, prioritizing ATEEVRA while preserving the marketplace non-destructively**.

The strategic dilemma in OD-002 is determining the **manufacturing and product sourcing architecture** for ATEEVRA luxury apparel. The house must decide how its garments are physically brought into existence:
- By purchasing pre-existing finished goods and relabeling them (White-Label),
- By commissioning bespoke contract manufacturing to proprietary tech packs (Full-Package FOB or Cut-Make-Trim),
- By adopting a category-specific hybrid sourcing model, or
- By building and operating an internal manufacturing factory.

## 3. Commercial Differentiation: Finished Purchasing vs. Contract Sourcing vs. Hybrid

Under the Project Owner\'s strategic directive to establish ATEEVRA as an authentic luxury brand, the sourcing analysis must rigorously distinguish between the commercial mechanisms:

### 3.1 Purchasing Pre-Existing Finished Products (Option C — White-Label Sourcing)
- **Mechanics:** The company browses catalog sheets from wholesale garment manufacturers, orders bulk ready-to-wear lots, and requests the placement of ATEEVRA neck labels, wash-care tags, and branded buttons.
- **Commercial Assessment for Luxury:**
  - *Advantages:* Minimal upfront design investment; low MOQs (20–50 units); fast turnaround (14–21 days); lower working capital commitment.
  - *Fatal Flaws for Luxury Brand Aura:* High-net-worth clients immediately detect standard off-the-shelf fit profiles, synthetic fabric blends, plastic buttons, and low stitch densities (10–12 SPI). Furthermore, competitors can buy the exact same blank from the manufacturer, completely destroying exclusivity.
  - *Legal / Metrology Reality:* Legal Metrology requires declaring the actual third-party manufacturer on the packaging, exposing the white-label source.

### 3.2 Commissioning Bespoke Contract Manufacturing (Option A — FOB & Option B — CMT)
- **Mechanics:** ATEEVRA\'s creative studio develops proprietary Tech Packs, millimeter-accurate grading tables, and fabric quality requirements. ATEEVRA contracts master tailoring ateliers (specializing in bespoke sartorial construction) to manufacture garments to exact specifications.
- **Commercial Assessment for Luxury:**
  - *Full-Package FOB (Option A):* Atelier procures certified luxury fabrics from approved mills (e.g., Raymond, Reid & Taylor, Italian Biella mills) and delivers finished garments to `WH01`. Atelier carries procurement risk; ATEEVRA inspects against AQL 2.5 before accepting.
  - *Cut-Make-Trim CMT (Option B):* ATEEVRA directly purchases fabric bolts from mills and sends them to the atelier purely for cutting and stitching. Provides granular fabric cost transparency, but increases internal supply chain complexity and carries raw material inventory risk.

### 3.3 Category-Specific Hybrid Sourcing (Option D)
- **Mechanics:** Rather than enforcing a rigid single manufacturing model across all product lines, ATEEVRA stratifies sourcing by **garment complexity and luxury visibility**:
  1. *Core High-Visibility Tailored Apparel (Suits, Blazers, Evening Trousers, Mulberry Silk Shirts):* **Option A / Contract FOB.** Built to proprietary tech packs by master ateliers in suiting clusters (Bengaluru, Noida). Zero compromise on luxury tailoring.
  2. *Luxury Knits & Casual Staples (Cashmere sweaters, Supima cotton polos):* **Certified Contract Knitwear Specialists.** Procured from specialized circular knitters with custom color dye lots.
  3. *Complementary Accessories (Silk ties, pocket squares, leather belts, cardholders):* **Certified Private-Label Artisans.** High-end boutique craftsmen who produce standard luxury leather/silk blanks customized with ATEEVRA embossing and packaging.
- **Commercial Merit:** Delivers maximum luxury aura on signature pieces while conserving working capital and reducing lead times on secondary items.

## 7. Strategic Recommendation with Transparent Reasoning

### Primary Recommendation:
**`OPTION A — CERTIFIED CONTRACT ATELIER B2B MANUFACTURING (FOB / FULL-PACKAGE)`**  
*(with consideration of **Option D: Category Hybrid Sourcing** as collection scale expands)*

### Supporting Rationale:
1. **Preservation of Authentic Luxury Positioning:** White-labeling (Option C) is commercially incompatible with an India-first luxury fashion house commanding MRPs up to ₹24,500. Luxury buyers pay for distinctive design, hand-finished canvasing, and superior fabrics. Option A guarantees proprietary pattern IP and custom fits.
2. **Capital Efficiency & Low Fixed Overhead:** An owned factory (Option E) requires ₹1.5+ Crores in machinery/fitout CapEx and imposes ₹27.3 Lakhs/month in fixed burn. For a debut collection of 500 units, building a factory is an existential financial risk. Option A converts 90%+ of production costs into strictly variable unit expenses tied directly to confirmed POs.
3. **Sartorial Specialization:** Tailored suiting requires entirely different master skills, pressing machinery, and fusing equipment than silk shirtmaking or fine knitwear. Option A enables ATEEVRA to partner with the best specialized master atelier in each garment category.
4. **Category Hybrid Potential (Option D):** As ATEEVRA expands beyond tailored suiting into accessories (silk ties, pocket squares, leather goods), adopting Option D to source accessories from certified boutique private-label artisans preserves capital while maintaining luxury quality.
