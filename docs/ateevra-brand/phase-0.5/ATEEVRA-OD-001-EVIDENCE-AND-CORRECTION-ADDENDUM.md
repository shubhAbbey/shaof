## 2. Ind AS 115 Principal-versus-Agent Assessment: Control-Based Proof

Under **Ind AS 115 (Revenue from Contracts with Customers)**, an entity must determine whether the nature of its promise is a performance obligation to provide the specified goods itself (entity is a **Principal**) or to arrange for those goods to be provided by another party (entity is an **Agent**).

### 2.1 The Foundational Principle: Control Before Transfer
Paragraph 31 of Ind AS 115 defines control as the ability to direct the use of, and obtain substantially all of the remaining benefits from, the asset. An entity is a Principal only if it **controls the specified good before that good is transferred to the customer**.

### 2.2 Requisite Contractual and Commercial Facts to Sustain Principal Treatment
To ensure the Statutory Auditor (Gate G-CA) upholds Principal accounting without challenge during statutory audits:
1. **Master Manufacturing Agreements (MMA):** Contracts with ateliers must explicitly state that title to finished goods transfers to ATEEVRA upon warehouse delivery and AQL 2.5 acceptance (FOB/ex-factory terms).
2. **Customer Invoices:** Must name ATEEVRA LUXURY PRIVATE LIMITED as the sole seller, with zero reference to third-party manufacturing entities.
3. **Return & Warranty Policy:** Must clearly state that customer refund and warranty obligations are owed solely and directly by ATEEVRA.

## 3. Scrutiny of the "4.7x Revenue Comparison": Gross Reporting vs. Economic Reality

### 3.1 The Flaw in Confusing Accounting Presentation with Economic Superiority
In Section 8.2 of the OD-001 dossier, the comparison noted:
- Option A Net Revenue = ₹6,964.29 (100% of GST-exclusive selling price).
- Option B Net Revenue = ₹1,478.57 (25% marketplace commission take-rate).
- The ratio $\frac{₹6,964.29}{₹1,478.57} = 4.71\times$.

**CRITICAL GOVERNANCE CORRECTION:**  
This 4.7x ratio is strictly an **accounting presentation variance** under Ind AS 115, **NOT AN ECONOMIC ADVANTAGE**. 
- It does **NOT** mean Option A generates 4.7x more profit.
- It does **NOT** mean Option A delivers 4.7x more cash.
- It does **NOT** prove that Option A is a financially superior business model.

### 3.3 The True Strategic Rationale for Option A
The Project Owner must understand that **Option A is not chosen because it is cheaper or carries higher short-term contribution margins at promotional floors**. In fact, at the promotional floor (₹7,800), a marketplace model yields +₹472.57 contribution vs. +₹173.29 for 1P!

Option A is recommended for three entirely different, profound reasons:
1. **Brand Equity & Price Defense:** In luxury fashion, no premier house (Hermès, Chanel, Brunello Cucinelli) operates as a multi-vendor platform. Marketplaces inevitably suffer from seller discounting, counterfeit leakage, and inconsistent unboxing, which permanently destroy luxury positioning.
2. **Gross Margin at Full Price:** At standard full MRP (₹18,500 for the blazer), Option A generates **+₹8,579.46 in net contribution (51.9% of net revenue)**, whereas a 25% marketplace commission generates only **₹2,683.00**. Option A vastly outperforms at full price!
3. **Enterprise Valuation Multiple:** Established luxury brands trade at 3x–6x net revenue multiples, whereas e-commerce platforms trade at low GMV multiples.
