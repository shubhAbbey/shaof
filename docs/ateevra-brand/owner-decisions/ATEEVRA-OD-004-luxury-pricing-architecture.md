## 1. Background & Context
Luxury pricing signals prestige, craftsmanship, and exclusivity. Setting prices too low degrades brand perception, while improper margin engineering threatens solvency during seasonal inventory turns.

## 2. Problem Statement
What pricing methodology and retail markup multipliers should ATEEVRA establish across its core fashion collections?

## 3. Decision Alternatives Considered

### Option A: Value-Based Multiplier (4.8x – 5.5x on Landed BOM Cost) (RECOMMENDED)
- **Description:** Retail prices are calculated using a 4.8x to 5.5x multiplier against Total Landed Cost (TLC), targeting 78%–82% gross margins on net selling price.
- **Pros:** Ample margin buffer to absorb express air shipping, luxury packaging, and high-touch concierge support; positions ATEEVRA in the contemporary luxury segment (₹8,500 – ₹45,000).
- **Cons:** Requires strict adherence to landed BOM budgeting.

### Option B: Cost-Plus 2.5x Fast-Fashion Multiplier
- **Description:** Pricing products at 2.0x to 2.5x of manufacturing cost.
- **Pros:** High volume sales potential.
- **Cons:** Eradicates luxury positioning; insufficient margin to fund bespoke customer concierge, rigid gift boxes, or express air courier.

### Option C: Ultra-High European Couture Multiplier (8.0x – 12.0x)
- **Description:** Pricing products at ₹65,000 to ₹2,50,000 from day one.
- **Pros:** Maximum prestige signaling.
- **Cons:** High brand barrier for a new entrant in the Indian domestic market; low initial sales velocity.

---

## 4. Recommended Option & Justification
**Option A (4.8x to 5.5x Multiplier)** is recommended. It perfectly balances contemporary luxury market positioning (benchmarked against Massimo Dutti, Theory, and Nicobar) with robust gross margins exceeding 78%.
