## 1. Background & Context
Luxury garments require specialized technical execution: pattern symmetry, precision stitching (minimum 14–16 stitches per inch), hand-sewn buttonholes, and premium fusing. Furthermore, luxury brands face intense brand risk if associated with sweatshops or unethical labor.

## 2. Problem Statement
What supplier onboarding protocols, technical specification packages (Tech Packs), and social/ethical compliance standards must ATEEVRA enforce?

## 3. Decision Alternatives Considered

### Option A: 4-Stage Atelier Qualification, Master Tech Packs & SMETA Audits (RECOMMENDED)
- **Description:** Tier-1 ateliers must pass (1) Technical Capability Audit, (2) Pre-Production Sample Approval, (3) Social & Environmental Audit (SMETA 4-Pillar / BSCI), and (4) Contractual IP Non-Disclosure Agreement; all manufacturing driven by standardized digital Tech Packs.
- **Pros:** Guarantees flawless tailoring and seam strength; protects proprietary patterns; ensures 100% ethical, sweatshop-free production.
- **Cons:** Slower supplier onboarding cycle (3–5 weeks per atelier).

### Option B: Informal Sourcing Based on Photographic Samples
- **Description:** Contracting local workshops based solely on photo portfolios and phone agreements.
- **Pros:** Fast setup and lowest administrative friction.
- **Cons:** Severe batch-to-batch quality variance; high risk of delivery delays, fabric substitution, and labor violations.

---

## 4. Recommended Option & Justification
**Option A** is strongly recommended. Rigorous supplier qualification is the foundation of luxury garment craftsmanship and brand integrity.
