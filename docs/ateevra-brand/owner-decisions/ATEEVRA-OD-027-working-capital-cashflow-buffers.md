## 1. Background & Context
First-party fashion brands must fund fabric procurement and garment production up to 60–90 days ahead of retail collection drops. An unforeseen delay or mismatch in the Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC) can create severe cash crunches.

## 2. Problem Statement
What cash liquidity reserves, working capital credit facilities, and cash conversion cycle targets must ATEEVRA maintain?

## 3. Decision Alternatives Considered

### Option A: 26.5-Day Target CCC with 3.0 Months Operating Cash Buffer (RECOMMENDED)
- **Description:** Maintain target CCC of $\le 26.5\text{ days}$ ($\text{DIO: } 70\text{d} + \text{DSO: } 1.5\text{d} - \text{DPO: } 45\text{d}$); preserve unencumbered cash buffer equal to 3.0 months fixed OpEx plus 50% of outstanding PO advance commitments; secure standby bank overdraft against inventory hypothecation.
- **Pros:** Total operational resilience during seasonal inventory build-ups; zero risk of payroll or supplier default during pre-collection manufacturing.
- **Cons:** Requires higher equity capital retention in treasury.

### Option B: Zero Cash Buffer ("Just-in-Time" Cashflow Reliance)
- **Description:** Relying solely on daily customer sales inflows to fund outgoing supplier checks.
- **Pros:** Maximizes capital withdrawal.
- **Cons:** Extremely high insolvency risk; a single courier transit delay or lower-than-expected collection launch creates immediate payroll and supplier default.

---

## 4. Recommended Option & Justification
**Option A** is strongly recommended. Prudent treasury buffers are vital for maintaining uninterrupted production runs with master ateliers.
