## 2. Project & Business Model Overview

ATEEVRA is an India-first luxury fashion house positioning itself in the accessible luxury apparel corridor (MRP ₹7,500 to ₹24,500). The brand sells high-end sartorial garments directly to consumers through its proprietary digital storefront.

### 2.1 The Two Business Lines Operated by the Single Corporate Entity
Under the Project Owner\'s explicit strategic direction recorded on 2026-11-05 ([ATEEVRA-OD-001-DIR-001](file:///C:/Users/Shubham/OneDrive/Desktop/ecom_mvp/docs/ateevra-brand/phase-0.5/ATEEVRA-OD-001-CONSOLIDATED-OWNER-DIRECTION-RECORD.md)), a single Indian Private Limited company will operate two discrete business lines:

1. **Business Line 1 — ATEEVRA (First-Party Luxury Fashion Brand — First Business Priority):**
   - Direct-to-Consumer (D2C) first-party brand.
   - The company acts as the sole **Seller of Record (SOR)** for all ATEEVRA-branded merchandise.
   - The company designs collections, contracts fabric mills and manufacturing ateliers, takes legal title and inventory risk for merchandise, issues statutory retail tax invoices directly to end-customers, collects retail consumer payments, and manages fulfillment, concierge returns, and refunds.
2. **Business Line 2 — Existing Multi-Vendor Marketplace (Independent Platform Business):**
   - An independent e-commerce marketplace platform facilitating sales between third-party merchants and consumers.
   - The company acts as an **Electronic Commerce Operator (ECO)** under Section 2(45) and Section 52 of the CGST Act, 2017, and Section 194-O of the Income Tax Act, 1961.
   - The company collects payment consideration, withholds Tax Collected at Source (TCS) and TDS, remits net funds to merchant sellers, and invoices merchants for platform commission and payment processing fees (+ 18% GST).
   - This business line must continue operating independently without disruption, code damage, or silent deletion.

### 4.1 GST Registration Architecture: Single GSTIN vs. Business Vertical GSTINs
*[PROPOSED TREATMENT — SUBJECT TO CA DETERMINATION — NOT CERTIFIED]*
- **Statutory Provision:** Section 25(2) of the CGST Act, 2017 read with Rule 11 of the CGST Rules, 2017 permits a person having multiple places of business or distinct business verticals in a State to obtain separate GST registrations for each such place/vertical.
- **Dilemma for Determination:**
  - *Option 1 (Single GSTIN):* The company operates both the 1P Brand and the Marketplace under its single existing Karnataka GSTIN. All 1P outward sales (12%) and marketplace commission invoices (18%) flow through one GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B. All inward ITC from fabric, ateliers, packaging, servers, and logistics is pooled into one electronic credit ledger.
  - *Option 2 (Separate Distinct GSTINs):* The company applies for a second distinct GST registration within Karnataka for the ATEEVRA luxury brand business vertical, isolating the electronic cash and credit ledgers entirely from the marketplace.
- **Specific CA Determination Required:** Which registration structure is legally permissible, operationally optimal, and provides the best audit protection against cross-contamination of tax liabilities?

### 4.2 First-Party Section 31 Tax Invoicing Mechanics
*[PROPOSED TREATMENT — SUBJECT TO CA DETERMINATION — NOT CERTIFIED]*
- **Applicable Rules:** Section 31 of the CGST Act, 2017 read with Rule 46 of the CGST Rules, 2017 (Tax Invoice rules).
- **ATEEVRA 1P Invoicing Model:**
  - For every customer retail sale, ATEEVRA issues a statutory tax invoice displaying:
    - Registered Company Legal Name, Trade Name (ATEEVRA), Corporate Office Address, GSTIN.
    - Unique sequential invoice number not exceeding 16 characters (e.g., ATV-2627-000001).
    - Date of invoice and Place of Supply (State of customer shipping address).
    - Customer Name and Shipping/Billing Address (B2C consumer invoice).
    - Accurate 6-digit or 8-digit HSN code (e.g., HSN 6203 for men\'s tailored blazers/trousers; HSN 6206 for women\'s silk shirts).
    - Itemized breakdown: Gross MRP, Base Price, CGST + SGST (intra-state) or IGST (inter-state), each stated explicitly in rupees.
    - Applicable GST rate: 12.0% for apparel exceeding ₹1,000 per piece.
- **Specific CA Determination Required:** Review and certify the proposed ATEEVRA Section 31 tax invoice template and confirm compliance with Rule 46, B2C e-invoicing exemptions (or applicability if turnover exceeds ₹5 Crores), and Dynamic QR Code requirements under Notification No. 14/2020-Central Tax.

### 4.3 Marketplace Section 52 TCS & Commission Invoicing
*[PROPOSED TREATMENT — SUBJECT TO CA DETERMINATION — NOT CERTIFIED]*
- **Applicable Rules:** Section 52 of the CGST Act, 2017 (Collection of Tax at Source by ECO) and Section 194-O of the Income Tax Act, 1961.
- **Current Marketplace Mechanics:**
  - For sales made by independent merchants on the platform, the marketplace collects the gross customer consideration.
  - The marketplace withholds 1.0% TCS (0.5% CGST + 0.5% SGST or 1.0% IGST) under Section 52 and remits it via monthly Form GSTR-8.
  - The marketplace withholds 1.0% TDS under Section 194-O.
  - The marketplace issues a Section 31 tax invoice to the merchant for its marketplace commission fee (e.g., 10%) plus 18% GST (HSN 9983).
- **1P Brand Decoupling:**
  - On the ATEEVRA D2C storefront, the company is selling its own goods. There is no third-party merchant.
- **Specific CA Determination Required:** Issue formal confirmation that:
  1. Section 52 TCS is **STRICTLY INAPPLICABLE** to ATEEVRA direct 1P sales, as no amount is collected on behalf of third-party suppliers.
  2. Operating both businesses under one legal company does not create a statutory presumption that 1P sales are marketplace transactions subject to TCS.

### 4.4 Inward Input Tax Credit (ITC) Mechanics & GSTR-2B Reconciliation
*[PROPOSED TREATMENT — SUBJECT TO CA DETERMINATION — NOT CERTIFIED]*
- **Applicable Rules:** Section 16 (Eligibility and conditions for taking ITC), Section 17(5) (Blocked credits), and Rule 36(4) of the CGST Rules.
- **Projected Inward Sourcing ITC Streams (Debut Capsule — 500 Units):**
  - *Finished Apparel / CMT Sourcing:*
    - Under FOB Option: 500 units @ ₹16,01,000 net landed cost + 12% GST = ₹1,92,120 ITC (HSN 6203/6206).
    - Under CMT Option: Fabrics @ 5% GST (HSN 5007/5111) + CMT stitching services @ 5% GST (HSN 9988).
  - *Embedded Inbound Freight:* ₹59,000 net + 5% GST (or 18% if Courier/GTA under forward charge) = ₹2,950 to ₹10,620 ITC.
  - *Luxury Packaging (Boxes, Dustbags, Hangtags):* ₹1,10,000 net (500 units @ ₹220) + 18% GST = ₹19,800 ITC (HSN 4819).
  - *Payment Gateway MDR:* 2.0% on ₹7,800 gross sales = ₹156 net fee + 18% GST = ₹28.08 ITC per transaction (HSN 9971).
  - *Central Warehouse Lease:* Commercial rent @ 18% GST.
- **Specific CA Determination Required:**
  1. Confirm that all modeled input tax streams are 100% eligible for ITC under Section 16 and not restricted by Section 17(5).
  2. Confirm whether the inverted duty structure applies if fabrics (5%) and CMT (5%) are utilized to produce finished apparel sold at 12%, or whether this generates an unproblematic positive tax absorption.
  3. Validate the monthly GSTR-2B automated matching workflow and rules for handling supplier invoice delays.

## 5. Direct Tax & Financial Accounting Matters for CA Determination

### 5.1 Ind AS 115 — Revenue Recognition: Principal vs. Agent Assessment
*[PROPOSED TREATMENT — SUBJECT TO CA DETERMINATION — NOT CERTIFIED]*
- **Statutory Standard:** Ind AS 115 / AS 9 (Revenue from Contracts with Customers).
- **Core Principle:** An entity is a **Principal** if it controls the specified good or service before that good or service is transferred to a customer. An entity is an **Agent** if its performance obligation is to arrange for the provision of the specified good or service by another party.
- **Assessment of ATEEVRA 1P Operations:**
  - *Control of Goods:* ATEEVRA holds physical inventory in warehouse WH01, owns the goods, and directs their deployment.
  - *Inventory Risk:* ATEEVRA bears 100% inventory risk before customer delivery and during the 7-day concierge return window. If goods are damaged, unsold, or triaged into grade B/C stock, the financial loss is borne entirely by ATEEVRA.
  - *Discretion in Establishing Prices:* ATEEVRA unilaterally sets catalog MRPs, sale prices, and promotional floors without consultation with suppliers.
  - *Primary Responsibility:* ATEEVRA is directly responsible to the customer for fulfilling the contract and delivering conforming luxury garments.
- **Assessment of Marketplace Operations:**
  - The company does not take title to third-party merchant goods, does not bear inventory risk, and acts purely to facilitate transactions.
- **Specific CA Determination Required:**
  1. Issue formal sign-off confirming that ATEEVRA operates as **Principal** under Ind AS 115, entitling the company to recognize **100% gross retail order value as revenue** on its corporate P&L.
  2. Confirm that the marketplace platform recognizes **only net commission income** as revenue.

### 5.2 Ind AS 2 — Inventory Valuation & Landed Cost Capitalization
*[PROPOSED TREATMENT — SUBJECT TO CA DETERMINATION — NOT CERTIFIED]*
- **Statutory Standard:** Ind AS 2 (Inventories).
- **Landed Cost Model for ATEEVRA Debut Inventory:**
  - The project models landed unit cost by capitalizing:
    - Direct fabric and raw materials.
    - Master atelier CMT assembly labor.
    - Trims, buttons, zippers, linings, and canvas.
    - Inbound freight to warehouse WH01 (totaling ₹59,000 across 500 debut units).
  - The model treats the following as **period operating expenses (OpEx)** rather than capitalized inventory:
    - Central warehouse rent, utilities, and security.
    - Luxury outbound presentation packaging (incurred at order fulfillment).
    - Outbound express air freight.
    - Marketing, advertising, and concierge stylists.
- **Specific CA Determination Required:**
  1. Confirm that the proposed landed cost BOM capitalization method complies strictly with Ind AS 2 (FIFO cost formula).
  2. Validate the accounting policy for carrying Work-in-Progress (WIP) under CMT contracts (Option B) vs. finished goods inventory under FOB contracts (Option A).
  3. Formulate the Net Realizable Value (NRV) write-down policy for aged, returned, or triaged seasonal luxury apparel.

### 5.3 Shared Overhead & Inter-Departmental Cost Allocation Methodology
*[PROPOSED TREATMENT — SUBJECT TO CA DETERMINATION — NOT CERTIFIED]*
- **Scope:** In a single legal company, common overheads are incurred:
  - Corporate office rent, utilities, executive management.
  - Cloud server hosting (AWS/Vercel/Supabase infrastructure).
  - Payment gateway corporate onboarding and API retainers.
  - Enterprise software licenses (accounting ERP, communication tools).
- **Specific CA Determination Required:** Recommend an objective, auditable overhead allocation methodology (e.g., activity-based costing, headcount proportion, or revenue proportion) to prevent profit distortion between the luxury brand and the marketplace.

## 7. Concrete Questions Requiring Written CA Determination

The Chartered Accountant is requested to provide specific, written determinations for the following 10 questions:

1. **Dual Registration vs. Single GSTIN:** Under Section 25(2) and Rule 11, should ATEEVRA operate under a separate GSTIN as a distinct business vertical in Karnataka, or under the existing single company GSTIN? What are the tax filing, audit, and credit ledger implications of each?
2. **Section 52 TCS Immunity:** Does the operation of a 1P D2C brand within the same corporate entity as a multi-vendor marketplace create any exposure to Section 52 TCS liabilities on 1P sales? What specific ledger and documentation protocols must be maintained to demonstrate TCS immunity to GST auditors?
3. **Section 31 Invoice Schema:** Does the proposed ATEEVRA retail tax invoice template fully satisfy Section 31 and Rule 46 requirements? Are there any specific disclosure requirements for luxury apparel or e-commerce D2C sales?
4. **Input Tax Credit Legality:** Are all modeled input tax credits (fabric @ 5%, CMT @ 5%, finished apparel @ 12%, packaging @ 18%, gateway MDR @ 18%, freight @ 5%/18%) fully creditable against ATEEVRA output GST liability under Section 16? Are any credits blocked under Section 17(5)?
5. **Inverted Duty Structure:** In the event ATEEVRA adopts CMT Job Work (Option B), purchasing fabric at 5% and CMT services at 5% to produce apparel sold at 12%, does any risk of refund blockage or inverted duty complications arise?
6. **Ind AS 115 Revenue Treatment:** Does ATEEVRA meet all criteria under Ind AS 115 to be classified as Principal for 1P brand transactions, allowing 100% gross revenue recognition? What specific terms in customer agreements and supplier contracts are required to support this?
7. **Ind AS 2 Landed Cost Policy:** Is the capitalization of ₹59,000 inbound freight into inventory cost, while expensing outbound freight and packaging as period OpEx, compliant with Ind AS 2?
8. **Inter-Business Overhead Allocation:** What formal overhead allocation formula should the company adopt to allocate shared technology, hosting, corporate legal, and administrative expenses between Business Line 1 (1P Brand) and Business Line 2 (Marketplace)?
9. **Advance Payment Tax Treatment:** Under GST law, are advances paid to apparel manufacturing ateliers (e.g., 30% PO deposit) subject to GST at the time of advance payment, or is GST levied upon receipt of the final commercial tax invoice under Notification No. 66/2017-Central Tax?
10. **Statutory Audit Readiness:** What specific documentation and audit trail (e.g., ERP stock ledgers, GSTR-2B matching, bank payment reconciliations, debit notes for returns) must be established before commercial transactions commence?
